# 01 — Novelty Audit

**Question.** Has any published study already integrated (i) individual fossil age-at-death estimates, (ii) sample-size-conditioned "oldest known individual" reports as order statistics, (iii) explicit age-dependent preservation/recovery weighting, (iv) age-estimation uncertainty, and (v) extant-to-fossil degradation validation, into a single latent mortality framework?

**Verdict.** No single work integrates all five components. The components exist separately in four literatures.

## Component map

| Component | Closest prior work | What it does | Gap it leaves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual fossil ages → survivorship | Erickson et al. 2006 (Science), Erickson et al. 2009 (Anat. Rec.), Woodward et al. 2015 (Paleobiology) | Static life tables from histologically aged assemblages | No explicit observation model; no age-error propagation; no sample-size conditioning of maxima; no systematic recoverability analysis |
| Small-sample uncertainty + size-dependent fossilization | Steinsaltz & Orzack 2011 (Paleobiology 37:113–125) | CI on age-specific survival; mortality-rate ratio tests; one worked example of positively size-dependent fossilization transforming the inferred curve | No parametric mortality modeling; no order-statistic treatment of maxima; no assemblage-mechanism distinction; no validation against known extant schedules |
| Parametric survivorship fitting | Ricklefs 2007 (Biol. Lett.), Erickson et al. 2006/2009 | Weibull/Gompertz ageing fits to published curves | Fits observed distributions directly; no separation of latent vs observed mortality; no taphonomic weighting |
| Stationary-age-distribution requirement | Griebeler 2021 (Palaeontology) | Shows bone assemblages need not preserve SAD populations; models corrected SCs via fecundity + lambda function | No per-specimen data likelihood; no observation process; no age-estimation uncertainty; no maximum-order-statistic treatment |
| Order statistics / max reported lifespan | Møller 2006; Xia & Møller 2018; Moorad et al. 2012 (extant birds); "bias in maximum lifespan estimates" (Beukema & Meehan 1988, cockle) | Showed max reported lifespan scales with sampling effort; regression-residual adjustments | Not applied to fossil assemblages as an explicit latent-model likelihood; no joint treatment with individual ages; no taphonomy |
| Latent-age paleodemography (human skeletal) | Konigsberg & Frankenberg; Bocquet-Appel & Masset; Buckley/Holzapfel et al. | Latent-age mortality models on skeletal age indicators | Not developed for non-human fossil taxa; does not address order-statistic maxima or extant degradation validation |
| Fossil recovery simulation | FossilSim (Barido-Sottani et al. 2019 MEE); FBD identifiability results | Simulates fossil occurrences under preservation models for *phylogenetic* inference | Occurrence-time simulation, not within-assemblage age-structure/mortality reconstruction |
| Degradation validation | Steinsaltz & Orzack use simulation for power; malddaba provides extant truth curves | Simulation for CIs, not systematic "fossilization" of known demographic schedules | No published systematic map of which mortality summaries survive which fossil-like information loss |

## Narrowest defensible novelty statement

> We treat individual ages, grouped/life-table data, and maximum-only reports as three distinct observation types generated by a common latent mortality process; we separate assemblage formation (attritional vs catastrophic/standing-crop), age-dependent observation q(a), and age-estimation error; and we validate recoverability by degrading known extant demographic schedules (malddaba life tables) into fossil-like samples, producing an explicit recoverability map.

Components with precedent (must NOT claim): fitting survival curves to dinosaurs (Erickson; Ricklefs; Woodward; Griebeler); using Weibull/Gompertz in paleodemography; discussing taphonomic/collection bias (Steinsaltz & Orzack; vast taphonomy literature); life tables from fossil assemblages.

## Genuinely open methodological territory

1. **Maximum-only likelihood contribution**: p(M|θ,n) under observed CDF G_j — order-statistic likelihoods for fossil reports are not implemented in any paleodemography pipeline we located.
2. **Joint likelihood over heterogeneous evidence types** (individual ages + max-only reports + grouped tables) sharing a latent F — not present in prior work.
3. **Recoverability map**: systematic identification matrix (quantity × information structure) from extant-schedule degradation — no analogue found; S&O did power analyses only for rate-ratio claims.
4. **Standardized expected maximum E[M_n*]** as a reporting metric for fossil taxa — related to, but distinct from, ornithological adjusted-max-lifespan regressions.

## Risks to novelty to recheck at Phase 8

- Any preprint/2025-2026 paper implementing "Bayesian latent mortality" for fossil vertebrates (monitor).
- Konigsberg et al. latent-variable skeletal paleodemography may anticipate parts of the observation-model formulation in principle; cite and delimit scope (human skeletal indicators vs fossil vertebrate assemblages + taphonomy).
