Figure accessibility descriptions (alt-text), one per figure.

Figure 1. Flow diagram of the five-stage generative chain: a latent
mortality schedule produces ages, then assemblage formation (attritional
deaths versus catastrophic standing crop), the observation process q(a),
age-estimation error, and reporting scheme determine the recorded fossil
age evidence; dashed nodes mark processes external to the population.

Figure 2. Three panels. A: likelihood surface over Weibull scale and
shape when a juvenile-deficit truth is fitted under a wrong constant-q
assumption. B: estimated mean age at death rising monotonically as the
assumed standing-crop growth rate r increases, showing the estimate
depends on r rather than the data. C: weakly curved likelihood surface
from a single maximum-only report.

Figure 3. Heatmap of mean bias in the estimated median age at death for
the informed analyst across fossilization scenarios (rows) and sample
sizes 5 to 100 (columns), pooled over three extant schedules; warm
colours indicate overestimation and cool colours underestimation.

Figure 4. Stacked bar chart: fraction of informed-analyst scenarios
classified A (robust), B (conditional), C (weak), and D (not
identifiable) for each mortality summary; mean age at death and E[M_50]
are mostly A/B, the median and juvenile hazard mostly C/D.

Figure 5. Two panels. Left: estimated median age-at-death distributions
under four evidence models; model D (double-counted maximum) shows
spuriously narrow precision. Right: expected observed maximum E[M_n]
rising with sample size n, shown for the latent distribution and for an
observed distribution with juvenile under-recovery.

Figure 6. Three panels for Maiasaura. Left: histogram of LAG counts
dominated by the zero-LAG class. Middle: fitted latent survival curves
by model family under an attritional, complete-recovery reading. Right:
tornado-style sensitivity of the estimated median to analysis choices,
with marker shape denoting model family.

Figure 7. Three panels for the Bayesian Maiasaura demonstration. Left:
posterior survival bands against the empirical survival curve. Middle:
posterior distribution for the standardized expected maximum E[M_50]
with the observed dataset maximum marked. Right: posterior densities for
the model shape parameters.
